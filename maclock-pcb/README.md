# Macintosh Mini breakout PCB

KiCad 10 project for the board behind the Mac-shaped clock's screen. It holds the clock's front-panel parts (rotary encoder, two pushbuttons, speaker, power switch and battery harness) and links them to the computer through an 8-pin header. The same board serves the Raspberry Pi and ESP32-P4 builds. It also carries the PAM8302A speaker amplifier, a 5 V supply for the computer, a low-battery cutoff, an input chip that reads the dial and buttons, and a battery gauge (see [Design notes](#design-notes)).

Current revision: 2027.01. **It hasn't been built or tested yet, and the interposers and software that go with it aren't finished.** To build a working Mini today, use rev 2026.11: its files are at commit [`ea41efe`](https://github.com/wr/macintosh-mini/tree/ea41efe2434ef0056f3ba3236e3c8bb0ac9fd945/maclock-pcb), the PCBway project below builds it, and [step 2 of the build guide](https://wells.ee/projects/macintosh-mini/#2-connect-the-pi-to-the-screen-and-the-board) wires it to the Pi. Rev 2026.09 boards, the first ones that shipped, work but whine faintly with the Pi running.

For the case-side parts you'll print, see the [3D-printed screen bezel](../maclock-screen-bezel/).

Order rev 2027.01 with **red solder mask and white silkscreen**, so it can't be mixed up with the blue 2026.11 boards. The [PCBway project](https://www.pcbway.com/project/shareproject/W654223ASS41_Untitled_kicad_pcb_95cca7e3.html) builds rev 2026.11. You can [use my referral code](https://pcbway.com/g/AsfKU9) to get $5 off your order, if you want.

## Design notes

**Power.** J2 brings in the clock's battery, about 4 V at full charge, and the power switch on J1 is in series with it. After the switch (net `+BATT`), the battery feeds the amp and its regulator, the gauge, the input chip and the cutoff, and U4, a boost converter that makes 5.06 V for the computer (set by R7/R8). The 5 V leaves through Q1, a one-way switch driven by Q2, so a Pi powered from its own USB port can't push current back into the battery. The boost, its capacitors and the link's two GND pins have their own ground copper (net `GND_PWR`), joined to the board ground only at the net tie NT1 by J2, so the computer's return current never flows through the amp's ground. On the rev 2026.09 boards it did, and the amp shared the Pi's supply copper, which put a faint whine in the speaker: a 1 kHz comb from the backlight PWM, plus drifting tones from the emulator's load. NT1 is copper only, so it is not in the BOM. The amp takes its supply straight from J1, ahead of the boost, and its 1 µF decoupling capacitor sits right against its supply and ground pins.

**Low-battery cutoff.** U5 watches the battery. When it falls below 3.08 V, U5 pulls the boost's enable line down; after about 2.5 s (set by C16/C19) the Q3 latch holds it down, so the computer and the amp stay off until the power switch is turned off and back on. The switch has to stay off for several seconds (about 10 s by calculation) while C16/C19 drain, or the latch catches again at once. The delay keeps U5's reset pulse at power-up (up to 560 ms) from tripping the latch. D1 clamps the enable line as those capacitors discharge at switch-off. The timings are calculated and haven't been measured on a board yet.

**Audio input.** The Pi's audio is PWM on GPIO 19, on the link's AUDIO pin, switching between the Pi's own 3.3 V and ground, so it carries whatever noise the Pi's supply and ground do. U2 rebuilds it from U3, a separate low-noise 3.0 V regulator (net `+3V0`), so only the timing of the Pi's signal reaches the amp. U3 runs from the clock's battery, which is about 4 V at full charge and falls as it drains, so it is a 3.0 V part rather than 3.3 V: that keeps it far enough above its dropout to keep filtering as the battery runs down. R3/C1 then low-pass it at 16 kHz. R15 holds AUDIO low when nothing drives it, as in the ESP32-P4 build, which plays sound through its own board.

**Amplifier.** The filtered signal goes C2 → R4 → PAM8302A IN+, with IN− terminated through the matching R5/C3 pair. R4 adds to the chip's internal 10 kΩ input resistor, which brings its fixed 23.5 dB gain down to about 8 dB; fine volume is a software setting, and `setup.sh` leaves 4 dB of headroom so the startup chime's peaks don't clip. The shutdown pin is pulled up through R6 from the boost's enable line, so the amp is on whenever the computer is powered and the cutoff turns it off too.

C6 is a spare pad, not fitted. R3/C1 is a single pole, which takes only about 24 dB off the Pi's PWM noise near the amp's own 200–300 kHz switching frequency, where a class-D amp can fold it back into the audio band as hiss or a faint whistle. A 470 pF across IN+ and IN− adds a second pole at about 20 kHz (with R4/R5 and the chip's internal 10 kΩ), for roughly 46 dB there, at the cost of about 1 dB at 10 kHz. The rev 2026.09 boards have the same single pole and none of their measured noise came from it, so solder C6 on only if a board hisses or whistles with the amp warm.

**Dial and buttons.** ENC1's A and B and the two buttons go straight to U7, a TCA9554 input chip (P0 dial B, P1 dial A, P2 SW2, P3 SW1; P4–P7 unused). Its own 100 kΩ pull-ups hold the lines high, and it pulls the link's INT pin low when one of them changes. ENC1 lies on its back with its bore over the dial's shaft and its pins pointing north; its footprint origin is the bore. HUANO publishes no drawing, so the land is drawn from a measured part: the bore is 4.5 mm from the pin edge and the pins are 3 mm long, and the pads run from just outside the body to the pin tips. Lying flat, it stands 4.15 mm tall.

**Battery gauge.** U6 measures the battery and reports its charge. It runs from the switched battery: its datasheet says it may shut down when SDA and SCL sit low for over 2.5 s, which they do while the computer is off, and powered from before the switch it would then miss the charging.

**I²C.** The computer reads U6 and U7 over SDA and SCL on the link. This board has no pull-ups on SDA, SCL or INT: the computer's side provides them (the Pi interposer or the ESP32-P4 board). U7 runs from the battery too. Its I²C inputs need 0.7 × its supply to read high, which is 2.94 V with a full battery, so the 3.3 V bus still works.

**Libraries.** Two project libraries ship with the board, next to the KiCad 10 standard libraries:

- `maclock` — ENC1's symbol and footprint, drawn for this project because the encoder lies on its back and has no published land pattern (there is no 3D model for it); symbols for U4 and U6; and L1's footprint, drawn to Murata's recommended land. A rule area on the board keeps tracks, vias and pour off the back layer under L1's body.
- `lcsc` — LCSC's own symbol, footprint and 3D model for SW1/SW2 (C2888448), imported with `easyeda2kicad --full --lcsc_id=C2888448`. easyeda2kicad's conversion artefacts were fixed, nothing else: the plastic-peg holes are NPTH, the bracket pads have no outline stroke (so the copper is LCSC's 1.5 mm), the pins are passive, and the courtyard is one closed outline.

## Fabrication files

Gerbers, drill files, the placement file and the BOM are generated with `kicad-cli` into `production/`, which git ignores:

```sh
K=/Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/MacOS/kicad-cli
mkdir -p production/gerbers
$K pcb export gerbers -l "F.Cu,B.Cu,F.Paste,B.Paste,F.Silkscreen,B.Silkscreen,F.Mask,B.Mask,Edge.Cuts" -o production/gerbers/ maclock-breakout.kicad_pcb
$K pcb export drill --format excellon --excellon-separate-th -o production/gerbers/ maclock-breakout.kicad_pcb
(cd production && zip -qj maclock-breakout-gerbers.zip gerbers/* && rm -r gerbers)
$K pcb export pos --format csv --units mm --side both -o production/maclock-breakout-positions.csv maclock-breakout.kicad_pcb
$K sch export bom --fields 'Reference,${QUANTITY},Value,Footprint,Manufacturer,MPN,LCSC,Supplier,Supplier Link' \
  --group-by 'Value,Footprint,MPN' -o production/maclock-breakout-bom.csv maclock-breakout.kicad_sch
```
